## Potential Final Project Directions

### Option 1: Comparative Taphonomy & Taxonomy - Almond Formation (Wyoming) vs. Judith River Formation (Montana)

**Goal:** Determine whether two Late Cretaceous vertebrate microfossil bonebeds (VMBs) differ significantly in taxonomic composition and taphonomic (shape) signatures, and what that reveals about their depositional environments.

**Data:** Taxon and shape counts from the Almond Formation (Turtle Hill, WY), collected during a 2026 Keck Geology Consortium REU I was part of; I will aim to use comparative data from the Judith River Formation (MT), which is from a previous Keck REU project. 

**Key questions:**
- Does taxonomic composition differ significantly between the two localities, and which taxa drive that difference?
- Does taphonomic shape composition (equidimensional, elongated, conical, flat) differ between localities?
- What do these differences suggest about depositional energy and environment at each site?

### Option 2: Is Syria's 2006–2010 Drought Unprecedented? A Deep-Time Perspective

**Goal:** Determine whether the severe drought Syria experienced in 2006–2010 is unusual in the context of thousands of years of natural climate variability, using a paleoclimate proxy record.

**Data:** Modern precipitation/drought index data for the Fertile Crescent; a regional paleoclimate proxy record (Soreq Cave speleothem oxygen-isotope record, Gejkar Cave, NOAA Palaeoclimatology database).

**Key questions:**
- How does the magnitude of the 2006–2010 drought compare to natural variability recorded across the Holocene?
- Are there past periods of comparable severity, and how long did they persist?
- Does the rate of change in the modern record stand out relative to natural rates of change in the proxy record?

### Option 3: Is the Walla Walla River Basin's Water Supply Changing Over Time?

**Goal:** Determine whether streamflow in the Walla Walla River basin shows a statistically significant long-term trend, with implications for local agriculture and water planning.

**Data:** USGS stream gauge data for the Walla Walla River basin; NOAA precipitation data for the region.

**Key questions:**
- Has streamflow in the basin significantly increased, decreased, or stayed flat over the available record?
- Does streamflow track regional precipitation trends, or are other factors involved?
- What might a detected trend mean for local agriculture (wine, wheat) and water resource planning?
