# Week 9 – Final Project Directions and Proposal

## Potential Final Project Directions

### Option 1: Comparative Taphonomy and Taxonomy – Almond Formation (Wyoming) vs. Judith River Formation (Montana)

**Goal:** Determine whether two Late Cretaceous vertebrate microfossil bonebeds (VMBs) differ significantly in taxonomic composition and taphonomic (shape) signatures, and what those differences reveal about their depositional environments.

**Data:** Taxon and shape counts from the Almond Formation (Turtle Hill, Wyoming), collected during a 2026 Keck Geology Consortium research experience I participated in. I would aim to obtain comparative data from the Judith River Formation (Montana), collected during a previous Keck research project.

**Key questions:**

- Does taxonomic composition differ significantly between the two localities, and which taxa drive that difference?
- Does taphonomic shape composition (equidimensional, elongated, conical, flat) differ between localities?
- What do these differences suggest about depositional energy and environment at each site?

---

### Option 2: Is Syria's 2006–2010 Drought Unprecedented? A Deep-Time Perspective

**Goal:** Determine whether the severe drought Syria experienced in 2006–2010 was unusual in the context of thousands of years of natural climate variability, using paleoclimate proxy records.

**Data:** Modern precipitation and drought-index data for the Fertile Crescent, alongside regional paleoclimate proxy records from Soreq and Gejkar caves, obtained through the NOAA Paleoclimatology database.

**Key questions:**

- How does the magnitude of the 2006–2010 drought compare with natural variability recorded across the Holocene?
- Are there past periods of comparable severity, and how long did they persist?
- Does the rate of change in the modern record stand out relative to natural rates of change in the proxy record?

---

### Option 3: Is the Walla Walla River Basin's Water Supply Changing Over Time?

**Goal:** Determine whether streamflow in the Walla Walla River basin shows a statistically significant long-term trend, with implications for local agriculture and water planning.

**Data:** USGS stream-gauge data for the Walla Walla River basin and NOAA precipitation data for the region.

**Key questions:**

- Has streamflow in the basin significantly increased, decreased, or stayed relatively stable over the available record?
- Does streamflow track regional precipitation trends, or are other factors involved?
- What might a detected trend mean for local agriculture (wine, wheat) and water-resource planning?

## Selected Project Direction

After further deliberation, I decided to pursue the second option and expand its scope beyond paleoclimate reconstruction.

### Is Syria's 2006–2010 Drought Unprecedented? A Historical and Modern Perspective

**Goal:** Determine whether the severe drought Syria experienced in 2006–2010 was unusual in the context of thousands of years of natural climate variability, using paleoclimate proxy records. I also want to situate the findings within the broader human context of population change, migration, agriculture, and water governance, which shaped the drought's impacts.

**Initial research questions:**

1. How does the magnitude of the 2006–2010 drought compare with natural variability recorded across the Holocene using speleothem records?
2. Is it possible to compare the modern PDSI record with relative moisture and aridity conditions inferred from oxygen-isotope measurements (δ¹⁸O)?
3. Are there past intervals in the proxy record with δ¹⁸O values comparable to or more extreme than those for 2006–2010? If the data's resolution allows, how long did those intervals persist?
4. Has the proxy record captured evidence of climatic shifts occurring as rapidly as the 2006–2010 drought?
5. Does the rate of change in the modern PDSI record stand out relative to natural rates of change in the proxy record?
6. Does the regional CRU TS air temperature record show warming trends, and could warming have contributed to the severity of the drought?
7. Did rural-to-urban migration accelerate during or after the 2006–2010 drought relative to the pre-drought trend, and did those changes coincide with declining agricultural yields?

**Proposed techniques:**

- Time-series alignment across mismatched temporal resolutions, including monthly and annual instrumental data alongside irregularly sampled paleoclimate records.
- Temporal aggregation and binning of paleoclimate measurements.
- Potential rate-of-change comparisons between modern and historical climate records, where data resolution allows.

**Scope:** While the statistical analysis will focus primarily on climate data, I plan to incorporate agricultural production, population patterns, and research on Syrian agricultural and water-management policies. These additional sources will help investigate how environmental changes interacted with human decisions and pre-existing vulnerabilities.
