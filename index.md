# Was Syria's 2006–2010 Drought Unprecedented?

### A Historical and Modern Perspective

**A CS-215 Data Science Final Project**

## About the Project

How unusual was Syria's 2006–2010 drought compared with earlier periods of natural climate variability? And how did the drought coincide with changes in agriculture, water use, and population patterns?

This project combines modern drought and temperature records for the Fertile Crescent with oxygen-isotope measurements from regional caves. It also examines Syrian crop production, irrigation, and population data to place the climate evidence in a broader human context.

The central challenge is to distinguish what the datasets suggest from what they can establish. Speleothem records offer a much longer perspective than instrumental observations, but their sampling and dating uncertainties complicate comparisons with a five-year drought.

## Where the Research Stands

The datasets have been acquired and preliminary cleaning is largely complete. Early analysis of the Gejkar Cave record suggests that 2006–2010 had a higher mean oxygen-isotope value than the historical five-year windows that met the chosen sampling requirement. This is an exploratory finding, **not proof that the drought was unprecedented**: historical coverage is uneven, and cave isotope values are indirect indicators of climate.

The next stage will compare the drought with other five-year periods in the modern PDSI record and explore changes in Syrian agricultural yields.
