# Was Syria's 2006–2010 Drought Unprecedented?

### A Historical and Modern Perspective

**CS-215 Data Science Final Project**

## About the project

How unusual was Syria's 2006–2010 drought compared with earlier periods of natural climate variability? And how did the drought coincide with changes in agriculture, water use, and population patterns?

I am combining modern drought and temperature records for the Fertile Crescent with oxygen-isotope measurements from regional caves. I am also examining Syrian crop production, irrigation, and population data to place the climate evidence in a broader human context.

The main challenge is distinguishing what the datasets suggest from what they can actually establish. Cave records offer a longer perspective than instrumental observations, but their uneven sampling and dating uncertainties make short-duration drought comparisons difficult.

## Preliminary findings

The 2006–2010 period has the highest mean δ¹⁸O among the Gejkar Cave five-year windows meeting my main three-sample requirement. At Gejkar, higher δ¹⁸O values are generally associated with drier conditions. However, gaps in the record and differences in sampling frequency prevent this result from establishing that the drought was unprecedented over the full historical period.

I am extending the analysis to the modern PDSI record and Syrian agricultural yields. The final project page will bring these findings together into a single narrative, rather than just a collection of weekly updates.
